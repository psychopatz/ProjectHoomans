# Dialogue Style Guide — Project Hoomans

## Purpose

This is the content-quality standard for the new variation-expansion job.

The target is not “more text.” The target is believable survivors whose dialogue remains semantically correct while sounding less canned.

## Core voice

NPC dialogue should generally feel:

- spoken, not written;
- human, not assistant-like;
- grounded, not theatrical;
- survival-aware when relevant;
- concise enough for in-game conversation;
- emotionally shaped by context/persona;
- relationship-aware;
- era/setting appropriate.

## Avoid bland robotic language

Bad:

- “I acknowledge your request.”
- “That is acceptable.”
- “I understand your concern.”
- “My current condition is adequate.”
- “Family.”

Better:

- “Alright. I can do that.”
- “Yeah. That works.”
- “I hear you.”
- “I'm holding up.”
- A complete relationship-grounded sentence rather than a semantic label.

## Avoid generic synonym spam

Ten variants must not be:

- “I'm okay.”
- “I'm alright.”
- “I'm fine.”
- “I'm doing okay.”
- “I'm doing alright.”
- “I'm doing fine.”
- etc.

A good ten-phrase pool should vary cadence and conversational framing while preserving meaning.

Example neutral wellbeing directions:

- “I'm alright. How about you?”
- “Still on my feet. You?”
- “Doing okay, all things considered.”
- “Can't complain too much. How're you holding up?”
- “I'm managing. What about you?”

Do not copy these examples mechanically into every family.

## Post-apocalyptic grounding

Use survival texture only when the semantic context supports it.

Good when appropriate:

- keeping watch;
- getting through the day;
- food/water;
- rest;
- wounds;
- shelter/camp;
- nearby danger;
- trust.

Do not insert zombies, blood, death or scarcity into unrelated dialogue simply for atmosphere.

## Relationship language

Never use:

- love;
- sweetheart;
- family;
- brother/sister;
- darling;
- best friend;

unless the semantic/bond context supports it.

Warm does **not** mean romantic.

Friendly does **not** mean family.

## Perspective

Always track:

- speaker;
- listener;
- subject;
- possessor;
- target.

Do not let NPCs accidentally describe the player's needs as their own or vice versa.

## Personality/style

### Guarded

Good:

- restrained;
- cautious;
- skeptical;
- clear boundaries.

Avoid making every guarded character hostile.

### Terse

Good:

- short but complete;
- natural contractions;
- minimal filler.

Avoid meaningless one-word semantic labels.

### Warm

Good:

- reassuring;
- open;
- appreciative.

Avoid unconditional intimacy.

### Neutral

Good:

- everyday spoken phrasing;
- clear and grounded.

Avoid generic chatbot acknowledgements.

## Combat/event dialogue

Keep it fast enough for gameplay.

Good combat lines usually:

- fit one breath;
- react to what actually happened;
- reflect trait/personality;
- avoid long exposition.

Do not write ten paragraph-length battle speeches just to meet variation count.

## Commands/tasks

Acknowledgements should differ by:

- willingness;
- urgency;
- relationship;
- repetition;
- current state;

only where the semantic family already encodes that difference.

Do not alter whether an order was accepted/rejected/queued.

## Identity

Do not leak private names/identity before trust rules permit it.

Name-response variations must preserve placeholder formatting exactly.

## Translation

TL should be natural Tagalog/Taglish according to existing project convention, not automatically English copied into the TL slot.

Preserve:

- placeholders;
- names;
- semantic strength;
- relationship tone.

Flag uncertain translation rather than silently inventing poor localization.

## Variation checklist

For every new phrase:

- same meaning?
- same context assumptions?
- correct perspective?
- grammatical?
- natural spoken rhythm?
- distinct from existing variants?
- style-appropriate?
- relationship-safe?
- setting-appropriate?
- EN/TL parity?
- placeholders intact?
